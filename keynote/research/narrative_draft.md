## WORKING THESIS (radical formulation, not "agility")

Working title for the thesis: **"The Sensing Deficit"** / **"Collective Perception is the new scarce resource"**

Candidate formulation (to test against research):
> For a century, organizations competed on their ability to turn a decision into an outcome — on execution. AI is closing that gap to nearly zero: any team can now produce, analyze, code, design and test at a speed and cost that would have been unthinkable five years ago. But an organization that can execute any idea instantly still has to know WHICH idea. That knowing is not a private act of one smart executive — it is a collective act: a system of people (and now machines) that senses weak signals, interprets them differently enough to catch what any one view would miss, argues them into shared meaning, and commits to action before the window closes. Call it **collective sensemaking capacity**. It was always the real bottleneck. Execution scarcity just hid it. AI hasn't created a new problem — it has removed the last thing that let organizations avoid solving the old one.

Sharper one-line versions to pressure-test:
- "AI didn't make organizations smarter. It made it impossible to keep hiding that they weren't."
- "Execution used to be the bottleneck, so it was also the excuse."
- "The organizations that win won't be the ones that move fastest. They'll be the ones that notice fastest, together."

## THE SIX THINGS THE THESIS MUST EXPLAIN
1. Why the world changed (permanent change as environment, not event)
2. Why traditional organization design (plan→execute, hierarchy, control) is running out of runway
3. What complex adaptive systems in nature teach about adaptation without central control
4. Why collective intelligence matters and where it breaks (Woolley/Malone + the groupthink counter-evidence)
5. How AI moves the constraint from execution to sensemaking/judgment/coordination
6. What has to change in organizations (leadership, structure, information, decision rights, culture, role of AI)

## 5 PARADOXES (draft — refine post-research, ensure originality)
1. "The cheaper it gets to execute an idea, the more expensive a bad one becomes."
2. "A room can get smarter one person at a time and stupider one opinion at a time."
3. "AI gives every individual a superpower and can still leave the organization exactly as blind as before — because blindness was never an individual problem."
4. "The fastest way to fall behind may be to move fast alone."
5. "We built organizations to protect decisions from being made too quickly. AI just deleted the delay. It didn't delete the reason we needed it."

## 5 QUESTIONS
1. "If your company could execute any idea by tomorrow morning, what would stop you from executing the wrong one?"
2. "When was the last time your best decision came from one person in one room?"
3. "Is your organization designed to optimize what already works, or to notice what no longer does?"
4. "How would you know — really know — if the market had already started to move without you?"
5. "What are you building that no one on your team could have imagined alone?"
