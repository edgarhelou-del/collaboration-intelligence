# Research: Collective Intelligence & Complexity Science (Agent A output)

## 1. COLLECTIVE INTELLIGENCE
- Woolley, Chabris, Pentland, Hashmi, Malone — "Evidence for a Collective Intelligence Factor in the Performance of Human Groups" — Science, 2010 — science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1193147 — "c factor" predicts group performance; correlates weakly with avg/max member IQ, strongly with avg social sensitivity, turn-taking equality, % women. Quote: "c is not strongly correlated with the average or maximum individual intelligence of group members but is correlated with the average social sensitivity of group members."
- Woolley & Malone — "What Makes a Team Smarter? More Women" — HBR 2011 — hbr.org/2011/06/defend-your-research-what-makes-a-team-smarter-more-women
- Woolley, Aggarwal, Malone — "Collective Intelligence and Group Performance" — Current Directions in Psych Science, 2015 — journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0963721415599543
- Credé & Howardson — "The Structure of Group Task Performance: A Second Look..." — J. Applied Psych, 2017 — CONTRADICTION: argues data don't support single general c-factor. Quote: "[data do] not support the inference that a general factor can explain substantial variation in performance across a wide cross section of group tasks."
- Woolley, Kim, Malone — reply to Credé & Howardson — SSRN 2018 — ssrn.com/abstract=3187373
- Kim, Woolley et al. — "g versus c: comparing individual and collective intelligence across two meta-analyses" — Cognitive Research 2021 — link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41235-021-00285-2 — mixed replication results
- Thomas Malone — "Superminds" — book, 2018 — hierarchies/markets/democracies/communities as superminds; AI becomes a new member, not a replacement
- Scott Page — "The Difference" (2007) / "The Diversity Bonus" (2017) — Princeton UP — "diversity trumps ability" but ONLY for hard problems w/ no dominant solver + good communication/shared goals
- Scott Page — "Diversity Paradoxes" working paper — faculty.wharton.upenn.edu — conditions where diversity bonus fails/reverses
- James Surowiecki — "The Wisdom of Crowds" (2004) — 4 conditions: diversity of opinion, independence, decentralization, aggregation mechanism — book itself covers when crowds go wrong
- Duncan Watts — "Everything Is Obvious" (2011) — common-sense reasoning about social systems is unreliable; wisdom-of-crowds more fragile than believed
- Salganik, Dodds, Watts — "Experimental Study of Inequality and Unpredictability in an Artificial Cultural Market" — Science 2006 — social visibility → runaway inequality/unpredictability in "popularity," weak link to quality. Quote: "best songs rarely did poorly, worst rarely did well, but almost any other result was possible."
- Lorenz, Rauhut, Schweitzer, Helbing — "How Social Influence Can Undermine the Wisdom of Crowd Effect" — PNAS 2011 — pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1008636108 — KEY: even mild social influence shrinks diversity of independent judgments, destroys wisdom-of-crowds effect w/o improving accuracy.

## 2. COMPLEX ADAPTIVE SYSTEMS & ORGANIZATIONS
- Donella Meadows — "Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System" (1999) — donellameadows.org/wp-content/userfiles/Leverage_Points.pdf — 12 leverage points, highest = paradigm/mindset a system is built on
- Dave Snowden & Mary Boone — "A Leader's Framework for Decision Making" — HBR 2007 — Cynefin: clear/complicated/complex/chaotic; complex domain requires "probe-sense-respond" not plan-execute
- Eric Beinhocker — "The Origin of Wealth" (2006) — economy as complex adaptive evolutionary system (differentiate-select-amplify), not equilibrium system
- Geoffrey West — "Scale" (2017) — cities scale superlinearly & open-ended; companies scale sublinearly & reliably die — structure determines whether growth compounds or decays
- Nassim Taleb — "Antifragile" (2012) — fragile/robust/antifragile; efficient execution can be fragile to shocks
- Ronald Heifetz — "Leadership Without Easy Answers" (1994) — technical problems (known solution) vs adaptive challenges (no playbook, must change values/habits). Quote: "The single biggest failure of leadership is to treat adaptive challenges like technical problems."
- Margaret Wheatley — "Leadership and the New Science" (1992/2006) — chaos theory/self-organization; relationships & information flow (not command-control execution) generate order

## 3. ORG LEARNING & PSYCHOLOGICAL SAFETY
- Peter Senge — "The Fifth Discipline" (1990) — systems thinking integrates personal mastery, mental models, shared vision, team learning. Quote: "In the long run, the only sustainable competitive advantage is your organization's ability to learn faster than the competition."
- Amy Edmondson — "Psychological Safety and Learning Behavior in Work Teams" — Admin Science Quarterly 1999 — 51-team study: psych safety (not team efficacy) predicted learning behavior → performance
- Amy Edmondson — "The Fearless Organization" (2018)
- Google Project Aristotle (~2015-16) — 180 teams: psychological safety > team composition as predictor of performance — corroborates Edmondson at industry scale

## 4. HUMAN-AI COLLABORATION / ECONOMICS OF EXECUTION
- Ethan Mollick — "Co-Intelligence" (2024) — "jagged frontier": AI excels unpredictably across tasks; bottleneck = judgment about when to trust it. Quote: "A landscape of sharp peaks where AI excels beyond human ability and deep valleys where it fails at tasks a child could master."
- Dell'Acqua, McFowland, Mollick, Lifshitz-Assaf, Kellogg, Rajendran, Krayer, Candelon, Lakhani — "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier" — HBS/BCG field experiment, Organization Science 2025-26 — 758 BCG consultants; inside frontier: GPT-4 raised speed >25%, quality >40%, completion >12%; but over-trust outside frontier hurt outcomes. papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321
- HBS AI Institute project page — aiinstitute.hbs.edu/navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier
- Satya Nadella (paraphrased) — "token capital vs taste" — AI scales execution ("token capital"); scarce resource = human taste/judgment. Quote: "Token capital scales the execution, while taste decides what to execute on."
- Sam Altman / Greg Brockman (Fortune, Feb 2026) — OpenAI hiring selects for "context, taste, feel for where field is headed" not raw technical skill. Quote: "We believe the best research teams are built through context, taste and a real feel for where the field is headed next."
- Jensen Huang — "Everyone is a programmer with generative AI" (CNBC/World Gov't Summit 2023-24). Quote: "It is our job to create computing technology such that nobody has to program... the programming language is human." Paired analysis: "AI lowers the technical barrier, but it does not lower the thinking barrier. In many ways, it raises it."
- Demis Hassabis — 60 Minutes/TIME 2025 — "radical abundance" framing — scarcity shifts from production to allocation/judgment. Quote: "That's what I mean by radical abundance. We no longer, in a meaningful way, are in a zero-sum resource-constrained world."

## 5. CONTRADICTING EVIDENCE
- Irving Janis — "Victims of Groupthink" (1972) — cohesive high-consensus groups (Bay of Pigs, Pearl Harbor) suppress dissent, produce worse decisions than individuals
- Ivan Steiner — "Group Process and Productivity" (1972) — process loss: actual productivity = potential − coordination/motivation losses ("too many cooks" formalized)
- Jehn, Northcraft, Neale (faultlines lit.) — diversity's effect is curvilinear via "faultlines" — subgroup splits along correlated demographics increase conflict, hurt cohesion
- McKinsey — "Three keys to faster, better decisions" (2019) — only ~20% of orgs excel at decision-making; 61% say ≥half of decision time used ineffectively; Fortune 500 co. loses ~530,000 manager-days/yr (~$250M) to bad decision processes
- Empirical info-overload study (2019-20, ResearchGate) — overload degrades decision quality/speed/manager fatigue
- Bogert, Schecter, Watson — "Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content" — Science Advances 2024 — KEY CONTRADICTION: GenAI writers individually better/more creative, but stories more similar to each other collectively. Quote: "Individually novel but collectively homogenous."
- Design Observer synthesis piece — AI homogenizing thinking, 2024-25
- Riedl & De Cremer — "AI for Collective Intelligence" — Collective Intelligence journal 2025 — individual AI productivity gains don't automatically aggregate into better group decisions
- Holacracy failures (GitHub, Medium, Buffer, Zappos) — radical decentralization produced coordination breakdowns, role confusion, overwhelm, not better adaptive judgment. "25% reported feeling overwhelmed by lack of structure."

## 6. INDUSTRY REPORTS 2023-2025
- Gartner — org design research — by 2026 20% of orgs use AI to flatten structure, eliminate >half of current middle-mgmt positions; by 2028 top performers cut decision layers 6→4
- Gartner — agentic AI decision-making — AI agents to make 15% of day-to-day work decisions by 2028 (from ~0%); half of business decisions eventually AI-augmented/automated
- McKinsey — "State of AI in 2025" — 88% use AI regularly in ≥1 function, only ~6% "AI high performers" (>5% EBIT impact); best predictor = workflow redesign around AI, not bolt-on. Quote: "Many local wins, little systemic reinforcement."
- McKinsey — "The Agentic Organization" (2025) — AI agents execute, humans concentrate on goal-setting/oversight/judgment
- BCG — "From Potential to Profit: Closing the AI Impact Gap" (2025) — AI leaders: 1.5x revenue growth, 1.6x shareholder returns, 1.4x ROIC vs peers over 3yrs; differentiator = concentrating initiatives + changing core decision processes
- BCG press release (Oct 2024) — 74% of cos haven't shown tangible enterprise AI value despite ~3/4 of CEOs being chief AI decision-maker
- Deloitte AI Institute — "State of GenAI in the Enterprise" Q4 2024 (2,773 respondents/14 countries) — "positive pragmatism," most run ≤20 experiments, <30% expected to scale in 3-6mo. Quote: "No matter how quickly GenAI advances, organizational change only happens so fast."

## SYNTHESIS NOTES FROM AGENT
- Strongest SUPPORTING through-line: Nadella/Altman/Huang convergent "taste/judgment scarce as execution cheap" + Heifetz technical-vs-adaptive + McKinsey/BCG finding decision/workflow redesign (not AI adoption) predicts advantage.
- Strongest COMPLICATING evidence: c-factor replication dispute (Woolley vs Credé & Howardson) — even foundational "groups can be smarter" claim is contested.
- Strongest DIRECT CONTRADICTION: AI-homogenization research (Bogert et al. 2024) + Lorenz/Salganik-Watts social-influence studies — cheaper/more visible individual execution can SHRINK collective diversity/wisdom, not guarantee better sensemaking.
